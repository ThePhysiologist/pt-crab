Reduce Cervicogenic Headaches and Dizziness with the Snag Technique

The Gist -SNAG (Sustained natural apophyseal glide mobilizations), a common method of reducing neck pain and stimulating proper joint glides, has long been used for this purposes, but can do even more. A study from the Journal of Physical Therapy Science showed that cervical headache snags and cervical rotation snags can significantly reduce cervicogenic headaches when used together in treatment. The combination of treatments works better than either on its own.

I Want More -The researchers split their subjects into three groups, one with cervical headache snags at C2, another with cervical C1-C2 rotation snags, and a third with both. (If you want to see the techniques, click them for video.) Outcome measures where the Neck Disability Index (NDI), the Headache Impact Test (HIT-6), and the Cervical Flexion Rotation Test (FRT), and the Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI). Everybody got better across every treatment, but the combined treatment group got significantly better than the other two in all but the DHI.

The group that got both mobilizations improved their NDI from 28 before treatment to 5 afterward, their Flexion Rotation Test by 46%, their DHI by 81% and their HIT-6 by 44%. The group that got just the rotational snags improved their DHI most, but only by 2% more than the group with both. Overall, the group that got both mobilizations did exceptionally across all outcomes, better than all the other groups.

The paper has some good speculation on why this technique is so effective and links to many other papers about cervicogenic headaches. It’s short and a good read.

Focus on Serratus Anterior By Raising the Legs in a Pushup

The effect of leg angle during push-up plus exercise on shoulder stabilization muscle activity
The Gist -A quick one here from the Journal of Physical Therapy Science shows how you can tweak a pushup position to tweak the muscular work involved. The researchers threw gonis and EMG machines on 15 young men and tracked their exertion when doing pushups. They found that raising the legs raised the effort of their Serratus Anteriors without changing what other muscles were doing. Legs up on a table, 110 degrees relative to the floor, showed the highest effect.

I Want More -This study’s generalizability is limited because of the small size and homogenous age of the participants, but it does indicate that increasing the leg height increases the work of pushups and the work done by the SA. This increase in SA work is due to the increased Ground Contact Reaction Force of the hands, as more body weight is pushed onto them as the leg angle increases.

Dry Needle Glute Med and QL to Fix Patellofemoral Pain

The Gist -Our last study comes from the Archives of PM&R and shows great value of Dry Needling QL and Glute Med combined with exercise for treating patellofemoral pain. Researchers split patients into two groups of 20 and gave one exercise to treat PFP and another exercise plus dry needling of trigger points in their QLs and Glute Meds for four weeks. They measured pain intensity, function, and pain pressure threshold at baseline and in weeks four and six, then compared the groups. Both groups showed improvements in pain, but the dry needling group showed greater improvements in pain function, and pain pressure threshold than the exercise only.

I Want More -The patients all received exercise 2x/wk at the clinic and 3 at home for four weeks, while the DN patients got 1x/wk needling at the clinic. Both groups got better at 4 and 6 weeks, with the DN group pulling away at both markers. Most notably, pain in both groups started at a 6 and decreased to about a 4 in the exercise only group and a 1.5 in the DN group, a big difference. The paper collected tons of data that you could parse through, but the result is quite clear: dry needling of Glute med and QL improves PFP pain. The one holdup is that patients weren’t blind to the Dry Needling, as there was no placebo for it. Regardless, the results look great, just keep that in mind as you treat.
